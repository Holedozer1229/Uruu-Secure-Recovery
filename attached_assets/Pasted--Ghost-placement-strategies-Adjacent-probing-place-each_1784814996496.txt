### Ghost placement strategies

- **Adjacent probing** — place each ghost qubit next to the data qubit(s) you want to probe so the entangling gate is a single two‑site operation; **minimizes gate depth** and reduces accumulated truncation error.  
- **Boundary placement** — put ghosts at chain ends when you want to probe global modes with minimal disturbance; useful when eigenmodes are edge‑localized.  
- **Interleaved placement** — interleave ghosts among data qubits to probe multiple local regions in parallel; increases entangling depth but gives richer branch information.  
- **Recommendation for Sweet16** — place the two ghosts adjacent to two spatially separated data qubits (e.g., ghost A next to data qubit 0, ghost B next to data qubit 7) to sample independent local sectors while keeping entangling depth minimal.

---

### Pulse placement and alignment to eigenphase splitting Δε

- **Measure Δε first**  
  - Extract two candidate eigenvectors \(|\psi_0\rangle,|\psi_1\rangle\) via time‑filtered TEBD or Arnoldi.  
  - Estimate eigenphases \(\varepsilon_k\) from the Floquet action on those vectors: compute \(\langle\psi_k|F|\psi_k\rangle = e^{-i\varepsilon_k}\) and take the argument.  
  - Compute \(\Delta\varepsilon = \varepsilon_1 - \varepsilon_0\) reduced to \((-\pi,\pi]\). Use this as the resonant frequency for swaps between eigenmodes.

- **Where to insert the pulse**  
  - **Between Floquet periods**: simplest; treat pulse as a separate short MPO block. Good when eigenphases are stable.  
  - **Overlapped with a period**: embed pulse layers inside a Floquet period to exploit constructive interference with the drive; useful when you want to leverage Floquet symmetries.  
  - **Recommendation**: start with pulses inserted between periods at stroboscopic times; once you have a working pulse, explore overlap to reduce required pulse strength.

- **Pulse design principle**  
  - Use a short sequence of two‑site gates whose net spectral content contains a component at frequency \(\Delta\varepsilon\). For small amplitude \(g(t)\) the effective coupling in the eigenbasis is \(\propto \int g(t) e^{i\Delta\varepsilon t} dt\). Tune pulse duration and phase to maximize that integral while minimizing off‑resonant leakage.

### Post‑selection and feedback strategies

- **Post‑selection only**  
  - Measure ghosts in the computational basis; accept runs with desired outcomes (e.g., ancilla parity = 0). This yields **low‑entropy branches** at the cost of reduced success probability. Use when fidelity is paramount and throughput is secondary.

- **Heralded feedback**  
  - Measure ghosts, then apply a conditional corrective pulse (MPO or local rotation) depending on the outcome. This preserves throughput while steering the state into the target eigenbranch. Requires low‑latency classical control.

- **Adaptive pulse conditioning**  
  - Use the measured branch to adjust the next pulse’s phase or amplitude (phase kick) so the subsequent evolution compensates branch‑dependent phase shifts.

- **Tradeoffs table**

| **Strategy** | **Fidelity** | **Throughput** | **Control complexity** | **When to use** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Post‑selection** | **High** | Low | Low | Proof‑of‑principle, benchmarking |
| **Heralded feedback** | High | Medium | Medium | Experiments with fast classical loop |
| **Adaptive pulses** | Medium–High | High | High | Long sequences, continuous operation |

### Practical measurement and feedback recipe for Sweet16

1. **Prepare**: run TEBD projection to get \(|\psi_0\rangle,|\psi_1\rangle\) and compute \(\Delta\varepsilon\).  
2. **Place ghosts**: put ghost0 adjacent to data qubit \(d_0\), ghost1 adjacent to \(d_7\).  
3. **Evolve**: run Floquet for \(t_m\) steps chosen where eigenmode separation is clean (monitor operator entanglement revivals).  
4. **Entangle**: apply two CNOTs (or controlled‑phase) from \(d_0\to\)ghost0 and \(d_7\to\)ghost1.  
5. **Measure ghosts**: read out in computational basis; record outcomes \(b_0,b_1\).  
6. **Branch action**:  
   - If **post‑select**: keep only runs with desired \((b_0,b_1)\).  
   - If **heralded**: apply conditional MPO pulse \(U_{\rm corr}(b_0,b_1)\) designed to map the measured branch back toward the target eigenbranch.  
7. **Apply resonant pulse**: insert designed MPO pulse tuned to \(\Delta\varepsilon\) immediately after measurement or after correction.  
8. **Tomography**: perform restricted Choi tomography on logical subspace to compute process fidelity and leakage.

### Concrete gate and timing primitives

- **Ancilla entangler**  
  - Use **CNOT** (data control → ghost target) or **controlled‑phase** depending on hardware. In MPS simulation use `cnot_on_total` or equivalent two‑site gate.

- **Measurement basis**  
  - Computational \(Z\) basis is simplest. For parity information, measure both ghosts and compute parity \(p=b_0\oplus b_1\).

- **Conditional corrective pulse**  
  - Precompute small MPO pulses \(U_{\rm corr}^{(00)},U_{\rm corr}^{(01)},U_{\rm corr}^{(10)},U_{\rm corr}^{(11)}\) by optimizing each branch’s diamond norm to the target logical identity or desired pre‑pulse state.

- **Timing guidelines**  
  - Choose \(t_m\) where operator entanglement is moderate (not fully scrambled) so ancilla coupling projects onto informative local features.  
  - Pulse duration \(T_p\) should satisfy \(T_p \cdot |\Delta\varepsilon| \gtrsim 1\) for spectral selectivity but keep \(T_p\) short enough to limit entanglement growth.
